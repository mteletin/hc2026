X4 math guest service

Enable X5 and the X4 math accelerator on a new machine. X4 is off by default. Load X4MATH.tap as a CODE tape at 32768, then call the entry at 32768 from your own program. The resident code, stack, request and 64-byte reply buffer must remain in caller-owned accessible memory. This explicit service does not patch BASIC or CP/M and does not intercept language operations.

Call with HL pointing at an 8–32-byte request, A holding its length, and DE pointing at the reply buffer. On return A is the admission code, FE for invalid length or incomplete reply, or FF when the card is unavailable. AF, BC, DE and HL are clobbered; IX and IY are preserved. An eight-byte refusal is followed by FF bytes in the buffer.

Frames begin with version 1, opcode, payload length, reserved zero, and a little-endian 32-bit tag. INFO (0) has no payload; START (1) has a 24-byte operation/format/options/operand payload; QUERY (2), CANCEL (3), ACK (4) have no payload. Non-INFO commands require a nonzero tag. START acknowledges admission, not completion: poll QUERY with the same tag until the state is terminal, read the numeric status and result, and ACK that tag before starting another operation. An ABORT of the transport does not cancel the engine; use CANCEL explicitly.

ABI 1 offers I32/I64 checked add, subtract, multiply and quotient/remainder; finite F32/F64 add, subtract, multiply, divide and square root; and explicit F32/F64, MBASIC single/double and Turbo Pascal real conversions. It does not advertise transcendental functions. Frames and results use little endian. START payload offsets 0–3 are arithmetic operation, source format, destination format and options; offsets 4–11 and 12–19 are operands A and B; offsets 20–23 are zero. Unused bytes and the second operand of SQRT or CONVERT must be zero. Arithmetic operations are 1 add, 2 subtract, 3 multiply, 4 divide, 5 quotient/remainder, 6 square root and 7 convert. Formats are 1 I32, 2 I64, 3 F32, 4 F64, 5 MBASIC single, 6 MBASIC double and 7 Turbo real. For conversion, option bit 0 enables rounding; otherwise options must be zero. Native formats are conversion-only. Arithmetic formats must match. DIV and SQRT are floating-point only; DIVREM is integer only.

A successful non-INFO reply has 56 payload bytes after its 8-byte header. Payload offsets 0–3 are state, operation, source and destination formats; offset 4 numeric status, 5 result length, 6–7 flags, 8–11 active tag, 12–15 latency/backend versions, 16–23 generation, 24–31 accepted tick, 32–39 due tick and 40–55 result. States are 0 idle, 1 busy, 2 done, 3 numerical error and 4 cancelled. The admission response byte is 0 OK, 1 busy, 2 invalid, 3 unsupported, 4 no target, 5 too late, 6 time limit or 7 resource limit. Numeric status values 1–8 mean divide-by-zero, domain, overflow, unsupported nonfinite, precision loss, native range, internal failure and invalid numeric encoding. Flags 1, 2 and 4 mean inexact, underflow and negative-zero loss. See the X4 ABI specification for exact validation, native encodings, rounding and modeled timing.
